* Help & Glossary

** What does this software do?
It is intended as a means of generating a printable set of round plant cards in a defined scale. These are used in the conceptual design and planning of permanent multi-layered plant systems: food, forests, forest gardens, and complex agroforestry systems. The cards representing the canopies of adult plants are a flexible means of positioning and mapping them on a project site. 
At the same time it is a way of organising a set of plants with their ecological niche data (key attributes, needs and tolerances, functions) and enrich them with your own information. These plants lists can be represented in different views or as calendars.

** A quick explanation of concepts, views and features.
*** What is a polyculture?

A polyculture is a group of plants that coexist in a defined space (patch). It consists of mostly perennial plants placed together in a manner that minimises competition and maximises cooperation between them so that they support each another: trees, shrubs, perennial herbs, ground covers, root plants and climbers are combined so they provide and share nutrients, light, water, pollination and pest control. Instead of a monoculture, a small, largely self-regulating ecosystem forms, often around a central anchor plant like a fruit tree.

** Pages/Menu

*** Plants
The plant list can be represented in various views, see below.

*** Calendars
The plant list can be ordered to show the flowering and fruiting times over the year.

*** Polycultures
On the Polycultures page you can create your own polycultures: pick an anchor species, add members with a desired ecosystem function (ground cover, nitrogen fixer, mineral accumulator, plants useful for pollinators and small animals) and edible yield (fruit, nuts and others). The suggestion panel filters your plant stock by unfulfilled functions and shows a light/water/pH compatibility score (0–3) in comparison to the anchor species.

** Views

*** Tiles
Overview with image preview, accent stripe by primary use, and a completion bar. Good for browsing.
*** List
Sortable table (desktop) or compact cards (mobile) showing with use-dots and completeness. Ideal for multi-select and bulk actions.
*** Cards
Print view of the plant cards — a preview of the PDF export in the browser.

** Card variants

*** Plant cards 
70 × 120 mm. One card per plant with image, name, attributes and phenology. Standard format for laying out, pinning up or collecting.
*** Strip cards
290 × 17 mm landscape. A compact row per plant — good for labels or quick overviews.
*** canopy card
round format with a graphical layout (layers, sun, water, pH, use, function). PDF in A4 portrait.

** Data sources

When creating or enriching a plant, data from several sources is combined. You choose which are active in the settings.

*** Wikidata
Botanical and common names, images, taxonomy. CORS-free, accessed directly from the browser.
*** PFAF (Plants For A Future)
Use scores (edibility, medicinal, material), light, pH and water requirements, growth rate, dimensions, flowering and fruiting times. Fetched via server proxy.

** Visual vocabulary

Filled badges = the plant’s uses (edible, medicinal, material…).
Outline chips = the data source a value came from (e.g. PFAF), functions as a link to the reference page.
Accent stripe / dot = primary use, shown on tiles and list rows.
Completion bar = how many fields are filled relative to the maximum (green ≥ 80%, yellow ≥ 50%, red below).

** Export & back up data

*** Backup (JSON) in Settings: 
saves the complete dataset including configuration — ideal as a backup or for moving to another device.
*** CSV / JSON export on the main page: 
saves individual or selected plants.
*** PDF export from the card view: 
all tiles, lists or cards.
